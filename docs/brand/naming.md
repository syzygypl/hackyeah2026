# Naming sprint: Airlock and Rój

Owner: pitch & brand (AI Mateusza). 2026-10-03, 12:20-12:45. Input for a human decision. Nothing is renamed in code until the team picks.

Method: about 30 candidates per product, 5 shortlisted, each shortlisted name checked by web search (existing AI/security products), DNS (domain hints) and meaning in PL/EN. No EUIPO/WIPO search was possible in the time box. Treat every "clear" below as "nothing found in 10 minutes", not as a trademark clearance.

## 0. The finding that matters most

**"Airlock" is already a security gateway sold to banks.** Ergon Informatik (Switzerland) sells *Airlock Gateway* (WAF and API security), *Airlock IAM* and *Airlock Microgateway*, "used in banks for years", 600+ customers ([ergon.ch](https://www.ergon.ch/en/solutions/airlock-gateway), [KuppingerCole](https://www.kuppingercole.com/research/ev81355/airlock-secure-access-hub-for-applications-and-apis)). A Goldman Sachs security judge may know it. This alone is a reason to rename Airlock. Rój has no such problem; renaming it is optional.

The AI agent guardrail space in 2026 is crowded: almost every English "control" word (Veto, Keel, Steward, Arbiter, Mandate, Custos, Tama) already has at least one agent-guardrail project. Polish words are the clearest space.

**1. Weto**
- EN: "Your agents act. Weto decides." PL: "Agenci działają. Weto decyduje."
- Why: every judge understands "veto" instantly, in both languages. It is calm authority, not a wall: the agent acts, Weto can stop it. 4 letters, 2 syllables. The Polish W spelling sets it apart from the English "Veto" products and signals "made in Kraków".
- Risk: the sound "veto" is crowded in agent security. In Poland "weto" is a hot political word in 2025-26 (presidential vetoes). On English slides, say it once out loud so nobody reads "wee-toe".

**2. Keel**
- EN: "Your agents move. Keel keeps them upright." PL: "Agenci działają. Keel trzyma kurs."
- Why: the best metaphor for "can act but can't go rogue": a keel lets the boat sail hard without capsizing. Same sound and meaning in Polish (kil). One syllable.
- Risk: an open-source "Keel" guardrail for coding agents exists. On stage "Keel" can sound like "kill", a bad word for a security product. Test it out loud.

**3. Śluza** (written `sluza` in code and URLs, like `roj`)
- EN: "Nothing crosses unchecked." PL: "Nic nie przejdzie bez sprawdzenia."
- Why: the literal Polish word for an airlock, so the architecture story stays the same and the team keeps its mental model. Cleanest conflict check of the shortlist. Two possibly free domains.
- Risk: weak for English speakers (no meaning, "ś" unreadable). In Polish it sits one letter from "śluz" (mucus); expect one joke.

**1. Wotum**
- EN: "Small models vote. No quote, no answer." PL: "Małe modele głosują. Bez cytatu nie ma odpowiedzi."
- Why: it says the mechanism (models vote) and the feeling (wotum zaufania, a vote of trust) in one word. Serious, not cute, known to every Polish adult. Cleanest conflict check of all names in this sprint.
- Risk: English speakers say "WO-tum", Poles say "VO-tum"; both are fine. "Votum" legal AI app is a near match.

**2. Sedno**
- EN: "The point of your letter, word for word." PL: "Sedno twojego pisma. Słowo w słowo."
- Why: it promises what a senior wants from a ZUS letter: the point, nothing else. Plain, warm, honest.
- Risk: loses the "many models" story. A Polish news app with the same name, also positioned as honest and anti-clickbait, which is close in tone.

**3. Lupa**
- EN: "Reads your letter closely. Says when it isn't sure." PL: "Czyta pismo dokładnie. Mówi, gdy nie wie."
- Why: the magnifier is the tool seniors already use for official letters. Same sound in English ("loupe"). Easiest to say of all.
- Risk: loses the swarm too. "Lupa" is also Google's magnifier on Polish Android phones, so it is hard to search for.

Note: keeping **Rój** scores the same as Wotum. It is the right call if the team values continuity over the family fit with the new Airlock name.

## 3. Recommended pair: Weto + Wotum

- Family: both Polish parliamentary words with Latin roots (veto / votum), both start with W, 4 and 5 letters, 2 syllables each. Related, not cute.
- One sentence for the whole team: **"Weto decides what agents may do. Wotum decides what they may claim."** PL: "Weto decyduje, co agent może zrobić. Wotum decyduje, co może twierdzić."
- Story fits the architecture: Wotum runs on Weto, every message in the swarm passes the same gateway.
- Both are understood by the GS jury (veto, vote) and by a Polish senior.

Fallback if the team dislikes the political sound of "weto": **Keel + Wotum** (no shared root, but Keel is the strongest metaphor), or **Śluza + Rój** (all-Polish, lowest conflict, weakest in English).

Before any real use: an EUIPO/WIPO search for the final pair in classes 9 and 42, and a decision on the Polish spelling in English materials.
